## Learning Activities | Repeated-Measures ANOVA

### Learning Outcomes

- Explain how repeated-measures analysis evaluates differences across conditions or occasions while accounting for measurements from the same participants.
- Interpret the pattern of condition differences in the context of the study.
- Evaluate the strength and limits of evidence about differences across conditions and their relevance beyond the observed measurements.

### Learning Task

> Study: A researcher measured four participants twice on an Outcome.
> - Outcome Time 1: 0, 0, 3, 5
> - Outcome Time 2: 4, 7, 4, 9

1. Use statistical software to obtain the descriptive statistics, the ANOVA source table, and partial eta-squared.
2. On your own, calculate the relevant elements of the within-subjects source table and partial eta-squared using the descriptive statistics given by the software. Verify that your calculations match the statistics provided by the software.
3. Using appropriate APA Style, report your results from this set of analyses.

### Discussion

- Why is it important that the two measurements come from the same participants, and how does repeated-measures ANOVA account for this pairing?
- What does the test of the time effect tell you about average change, and what does partial eta-squared add about the strength of that effect?
- What can two measurement occasions establish about change, and what can they not show about individual patterns, lasting effects, or the cause of the change?
