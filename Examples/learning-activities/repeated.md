# [Examples Articles](../index.md)

## Learning Activities | Repeated-Measures ANOVA

### Learning Outcomes

- Explain how repeated-measures analysis evaluates differences across conditions or occasions while accounting for measurements from the same participants.
- Interpret the pattern of condition differences in the context of the study.
- Evaluate the strength and limits of evidence about differences across conditions and their relevance beyond the observed measurements.

### Learning Sequence

- Identify the repeated conditions, quantitative outcome, and which measurements belong to the same participant.
- Locate the condition means and standard deviations; describe the sample pattern and use grand or marginal means where reported.
- Calculate or verify the hypothesis test by tracing the participant, condition, and error variability; locate the *F* statistic, degrees of freedom, and *p* value.
- Calculate or locate partial eta-squared to describe effect size. It is reported in several outputs but must be calculated from sums of squares in R. Calculate or locate confidence intervals for condition means as needed; only CREATE's annotated output provides them.
- Summarize the condition descriptives, test, effect size, and any reported confidence intervals in APA style.

### Discussion

- How might collecting measurements at more occasions reveal a pattern that a two-condition comparison cannot show?
- What other outcomes or follow-up measurements would help establish whether the observed difference is meaningful or lasts over time?
- How does accounting for participant-level differences affect the interpretation of a repeated-measures result compared with treating each score as independent?
