## Learning Activities | Standardized Scores

### Learning Outcomes

- Explain how standardized scores express observations relative to the center and spread of a distribution.
- Interpret relative standing across a distribution while recognizing that standardized values do not retain the original measurement units.
- Distinguish standardization from percentile interpretation and evaluate what additional assumptions are needed to connect the two.

### Learning Task

> Study: A researcher measured eight participants on an Outcome.
> - Outcome: 0, 0, 3, 5, 4, 7, 4, 9

1. Use statistical software to obtain the quartiles, mean, and standard deviations for the variable.
2. On your own, use the z-score formula, a normal distribution table, and the descriptive statistics given to you by the software to calculate estimates of the scores that would be at the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles.
3. Describe how the estimates derived from using z scores differ from the quartiles provided by the software.

### Discussion

- What does a *z* score tell you about a score's location relative to the mean, and what information about the original measurement units does it leave out?
- Why do the percentile estimates based on a normal distribution not necessarily match the observed quartiles, especially in a small sample with tied scores?
- How are standardized scores and percentiles different descriptions of relative standing, and what assumptions are needed to move from one to the other?
