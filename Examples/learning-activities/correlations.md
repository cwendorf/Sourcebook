# [Examples Articles](../index.md)

## Learning Activities | Correlations

### Learning Outcomes

- Explain how paired measurements on two outcome variables are used to describe their relationship.
- Interpret the sign and magnitude of Pearson's *r* in context, and recognize that the correlation is the same whichever variable is listed first.
- Interpret the test of whether a correlation differs from zero, including the *p* value and a confidence interval when one is available.

### Learning Sequence

- Identify the two quantitative outcomes and confirm that each pair of scores belongs to the same participant or unit.
- Locate descriptive statistics for each outcome and use them to characterize the paired data.
- Calculate or verify the sum of cross products, covariance, and Pearson's *r*; interpret the sign and magnitude of *r* as a description of the relationship.
- Locate the between-variable correlation in the output, recognize that correlation is symmetric, and interpret the test of whether it differs from zero using the *t* statistic and *p* value; include a confidence interval when reported.
- Summarize the relationship and its inferential results in APA style, distinguishing evidence of association from a causal conclusion.

### Discussion

- How might a larger sample change the precision of the estimated correlation and the conclusions from its significance test?
- What additional variables or a different study design would help explain why these outcomes are related?
- What would you want to know before using this result to plan a future study or make a claim about one outcome causing the other?
