# [Examples Articles](../index.md)

## Learning Activities | Correlations

### Learning Outcomes

- Explain how paired measurements on two outcome variables are used to describe their relationship.
- Interpret the sign and magnitude of Pearson's *r* in context, and recognize that the correlation is the same whichever variable is listed first.
- Interpret the test of whether a correlation differs from zero, including the *p* value and a confidence interval when one is available.

### Learning Task

> Study: A researcher measured four participants on two Outcomes.
> - Outcome 1: 0, 0, 3, 5
> - Outcome 2: 4, 7, 4, 9

1. Use statistical software to obtain the means, standard deviations, sum of cross-products, covariance, and correlation for the variables.
2. On your own, calculate the covariance, correlation, and the squared correlation among the variables using the descriptive statistics given by the software. Verify that your calculations match the statistics provided by the software.
3. Using appropriate APA Style, report your results from this set of analyses.

### Discussion

- What do the sign and magnitude of Pearson's *r* say about the direction and strength of the linear relationship between the paired outcomes, and why does reversing their order not change *r*?
- What does the squared correlation tell you about shared variability, and why does it not show that one outcome causes the other?
- What does a test of whether the correlation differs from zero tell you, and how should its *p* value or confidence interval be interpreted given the very small sample?
