## Learning Activities | Regression

### Learning Outcomes

- Explain how linear regression represents the relationship between a predictor and a quantitative outcome.
- Interpret the direction and strength of the relationship and what the model can indicate about expected outcomes.
- Evaluate the usefulness and limits of the model, including why prediction or association alone does not establish causation.

### Learning Task

> Study: A researcher measured four participants on two Outcomes.
>
> Outcome 1: 0, 0, 3, 5
> Outcome 2: 4, 7, 4, 9

1. Use statistical software to obtain the means, standard deviations, and regression model and coefficients for the variables.
2. On your own, calculate the correlation, squared correlation, and regression coefficients for the variables using the descriptive statistics given by the software. Verify that your calculations match the statistics provided by the software.
3. Using appropriate APA Style, report your results from this set of analyses.

### Discussion

- What evidence from a study design would be needed to support a causal claim rather than an association or prediction?
- How would reversing the roles of predictor and outcome change the fitted model and its use for prediction?
- What additional observations or predictors could improve the model or show where its predictions are less accurate?
