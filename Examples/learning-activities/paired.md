# [Examples Articles](../index.md)

## Learning Activities | Paired-Samples *t* Test

### Learning Outcomes

- Explain how paired comparisons evaluate differences between linked measurements while accounting for their relationship.
- Interpret the direction and pattern of within-pair change in the context of the study.
- Evaluate the strength and limits of evidence about change and what it implies for the participants or units studied.

### Learning Sequence

- Identify the paired measurements, the quantitative outcome, and which measurement is subtracted from which.
- Locate the descriptives for each measurement and for the pairwise differences; use the mean difference to describe the direction of change.
- Calculate or verify the hypothesis test from the difference scores, including the test statistic, degrees of freedom, and *p* value.
- Calculate or locate the confidence interval for the mean difference and Cohen's *d*. The signs of the mean difference, *t*, and interval depend on subtraction order; check the effect-size convention separately, since the R annotation reports *d* with a different sign.
- Summarize the paired descriptives, difference, test, confidence interval, and effect size in APA style.

### Discussion

- What information about within-pair change would be lost if the two measurements were analyzed as independent groups?
- How would the interpretation of improvement or decline change if the subtraction order were reversed?
- What additional measurement occasions or a comparison group could help explain whether the observed change is sustained or specific to the study conditions?
