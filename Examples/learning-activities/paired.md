## Learning Activities | Paired-Samples *t* Test

### Learning Outcomes

- Explain how paired comparisons evaluate differences between linked measurements while accounting for their relationship.
- Interpret the direction and pattern of within-pair change in the context of the study.
- Evaluate the strength and limits of evidence about change and what it implies for the participants or units studied.

### Learning Task

> Study: A researcher measured four participants twice on an Outcome.
> - Outcome Time 1: 0, 0, 3, 5
> - Outcome Time 2: 4, 7, 4, 9

1. Use statistical software to obtain the mean, standard deviation, and the one-sample t test.
2. On your own, calculate the one-sample t test, the confidence interval for the difference, and the standardized effect size using the descriptive statistics given by the software. Verify that your calculations match the statistics provided by the software.
3. Using appropriate APA Style, report your results from this set of analyses.

### Discussion

- Why does a paired-samples *t* test analyze within-participant differences rather than treat the two sets of scores as independent?
- What does the mean difference say about the direction of change, and how do the confidence interval and standardized effect size add to its interpretation?
- What can this test establish about change in these participants, and why does an observed change alone not establish its cause or persistence?
