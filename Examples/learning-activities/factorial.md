## Learning Activities | Factorial ANOVA

### Learning Outcomes

- Explain how factorial ANOVA evaluates the effects of multiple factors on a quantitative outcome, including whether their effects depend on one another.
- Interpret main effects and interactions in context, distinguishing average differences from patterns that vary across factor combinations.
- Evaluate the strength and limits of factorial ANOVA findings and identify appropriate directions for further analysis or study.

### Learning Task

> Study: A researcher measured sixteen participants on an Outcome. Participants were divided into four groups of four based on two factors (Factor A and Factor B), each with two levels.
> - Group A1B1 Outcome: 0, 0, 3, 5
> - Group A2B1 Outcome: 4, 7, 4, 9
> - Group A1B2 Outcome: 9, 6, 4, 9
> - Group A2B2 Outcome: 3, 6, 8, 3

1. Use statistical software to obtain the descriptive statistics, the source table, and the effect size associated with a Factorial ANOVA.
2. On your own, calculate the elements of the source table and eta-squared using the descriptive statistics given by the software. Verify that your calculations match the statistics provided by the software.
3. Using appropriate APA Style, report your results from this set of analyses.

### Discussion

- What does each main effect describe, and how is an interaction different from the separate effects of Factor A and Factor B?
- How can the cell means reveal whether the effect of one factor depends on the level of the other, and why can that pattern change the interpretation of main effects?
- What does eta-squared add to interpreting the effects, and what design or analysis limitations should temper conclusions about their strength and generality?
